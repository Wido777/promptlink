What's hiding for your AI?

Websites can slip instructions to your AI: inside "Summarize with AI" links, or in page text you can't see. Paste a link or a page's code to read exactly what they say.

How the trick works

  1. A site adds a "Summarize with AI" buttonIts link carries a summary request plus one line about the site's brand.
  2. You click itYour AI runs the whole prompt, including the line you never saw.
  3. Your AI remembers itLater it recommends that brand as if it had chosen it itself.

Microsoft's security team found 50+ of these prompts from 31 companies. Pages can do the same without a button: text hidden from visitors, in comments or image descriptions, that an AI reads when you ask it to summarise the page. promptlink catches the known patterns but can miss new wording, so read what it shows you too.

Keyword rules vs an AI check

Rules only catch wording they know. The command-line tool can also ask an AI model, running on your own computer, what a text is trying to do. On 22 attacks and 24 harmless texts written after both were frozen:

Attacks caughtFalse alarms
Keyword rules (this page)4 of 221 of 24
Rules + AI check (qwen2.5:7b, local)19 of 221 of 24

This page runs the keyword rules only, so nothing you paste leaves your browser. The AI check is in the command-line tool. Full method, data and misses: test results on GitHub.
